## What this is based on

survey, no soil/sun data). Everything below the "Site read from imagery"

## Site read from imagery

- **Structure:** single-story house with a red/terracotta tile roof, an
  L-shaped footprint (two roof planes meeting near the center), consistent
  with a Mediterranean/Spanish-style build.
- **Driveway:** curves in from the road (Breezeway Dr, south side of the
  lot) up to a paved apron/turnaround at the front entry.
- **Canopy cover:** the lot is heavily wooded — dense tree canopy covers
  most of the north, west, and east sides of the parcel, right up to the
  building line in places. A large tree canopy sits close to the
  roofline near the south/front corner of the house, with visible shadow
  cast directly onto the roof.
- **Parcel outline:** the boundary overlay (second screenshot) shows the
  buildable/parcel envelope as noticeably larger than the roof footprint,
  meaning the wooded area isn't incidental — it's most of the lot.
- **Neighboring structure:** a building is visible at the edge of the frame
  to the east/right, close enough that side-yard planting should account
  for a shared or near property line there.

## Not yet known — needed before any real plan

- State/climate zone (USDA hardiness zone), since plant selection depends
  entirely on this and the address alone doesn't confirm it
- Actual sun exposure per zone (the canopy cover visible from above doesn't
  say how much direct sun reaches ground level in each area)
- Soil type/drainage (heavy clay vs. sandy vs. loam changes almost every
  recommendation below)
- HOA or local ordinance constraints (tree removal permits are common in
  wooded/canopy neighborhoods)
- Budget and whether this is DIY or contractor-installed
- Which trees near the roofline are healthy vs. a removal/trim candidate —
  imagery can show proximity, not tree health
- Irrigation: existing system present or not

## Candidate work areas (from what's visible)

1. **Tree-to-roof clearance near the front/south corner.** The canopy
   overhanging the roof in the imagery is the one item worth flagging as
   urgent rather than cosmetic — overhanging limbs on tile roofing are a
   debris/moisture/fire-risk item independent of any landscaping goals.
   This should go to a certified arborist for a real clearance assessment
   before anything else on this list.
2. **Entry/driveway approach.** The curved driveway and paved turnaround
   are the obvious first-impression zone — foundation beds along the
   entry walk, a defined edge where paving meets the wooded area, and
   possibly uplighting for the tile roofline are typical treatments here.
3. **Foundation beds around the house.** Standard low-maintenance shrub/
   groundcover beds against the visible wall lines, sized to the
   sun/shade conditions once confirmed on site.
4. **Wooded perimeter as a managed buffer, not a lawn conversion.**
   Given how much of the lot is canopy, the practical approach is usually
   to manage the existing woodland edge (understory cleanup, native
   shade-tolerant plantings, a defined transition line) rather than
   clearing for turf — clearing that much canopy would also likely need
   a permit in most jurisdictions with tree ordinances.
5. **East side yard, near the neighboring structure.** Any planting here
   should stay conservative on spread/height given the visible proximity
   to the adjacent building.
