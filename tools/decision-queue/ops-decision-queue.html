Ops Decision Queue

The approval dashboard for AEGIS's "must-be-reviewed-by-Jeremy" gate. Ops raises anything it isn't sure it should call unilaterally, or that the classifier routes here directly. Items marked needs you stay open until you give a positive confirmation — nothing here auto-approves itself. Everything else is ops's call, already framed as its recommendation, proceeding unless you say otherwise.

Add a decision
Backed by this artifact's shared store — any session can add a decision via write_db on the decisions collection; resolutions land back the same way via read_db. Nothing here is a live action by itself — it's a record of what was decided and why.

Every card requires a bestPractice field (2026-09-16 standing requirement), distinct from suggestedResolution: suggestedResolution is what we'd do here; bestPractice is what established practice actually says, with its reasoning and source — name the specific MasterThread/standards/ or _security-public/policies/ doc if one applies, or the recognized general practice if it doesn't. If our suggestion diverges from it, say so explicitly in divergenceNote and explain why — that divergence is the most useful thing on the card, it's where Jeremy most needs to weigh in. Where genuinely no standard covers the situation, write that plainly rather than inventing one.

A card with options requires a recommendation (2026-09-16 standing requirement): recommendedOption (integer index into options), recommendedBy, and recommendedRationale. Ops makes the call, Jeremy reviews and approves or overrides it — a card with options and no recommendation pushes the analysis back onto him, which is exactly what this corrects. Cards without options (free-text resolution only) aren't affected. The recommendation is shown, never preselected (2026-09-17 owner decision, "No pre-highlight"): the recommended option carries an "ops recommends" label and the reasoning sits in the box above the options, but no option starts highlighted and Approve stays disabled until he clicks the one he wants. Every recorded answer is therefore a pick he made.

Write cards to be scanned, not read: summary + points (2026-09-17 owner request — Jeremy: "the new cards are big section of text, I struggle with that format"). Every card SHOULD carry summary (string): one plain sentence that is the question he is being asked, no jargon or internal shorthand, under ~200 characters — shown first as "In short". And points (array of strings, at most 5, one line each): only what he needs to know to answer — shown as the "What matters" bullets. Any link or path he must use goes on its own bullet (URLs become links, Windows paths get a Copy button). The long explanation goes in details (or legacy context) and is shown collapsed under "Background and sources" at the bottom of the card, line breaks kept. bestPractice stays required and divergenceNote still applies, but both are shown inside that same collapsed section, along with any corrections. Keep each options label short enough to read at a glance (aim under ~90 characters); the reasoning belongs in recommendedRationale, which stays visible, not in the label. Action cards are the one exception: their context is the numbered steps, so it stays visible — add summary on top. Cards without summary / points still render — the first sentence of context stands in as the summary and the rest sits in the collapsed section — so nothing breaks, it just reads worse.

Approval device lock, honestly: resolving an item is only enabled on the one browser paired below, and disabled on anything the page detects as a phone/tablet. That's a client-side speed bump against an accidental phone tap while headless, not real security — anyone with DevTools access to the paired browser (or its local storage) could still bypass it, and there is no server-side check behind it. Treat it as a policy nudge, not a lock.

Confirming any action is two clicks, in-page — never a native browser dialog. (2026-09-16 fix: the first version used window.confirm(), which a sandboxed artifact view can silently block — the click did nothing, with no visible error, which is exactly as confusing as it sounds. 2026-09-17: this was initially only wired to the "approve recommended" button; every button that writes a resolution or override now arms/confirms the same way — Approve, Decide differently, Resolve, Mark reviewed as-is, and Submit override all included.) The first click arms it (button turns red, reads "Click again to confirm", disarms itself after 8 seconds if you don't follow through, and any armed button on a card disarms automatically if you pick a different option first); the second click actually writes the resolution. No modal, nothing that depends on the browser allowing popups/dialogs in this context. The option you picked is remembered per card while the page refreshes itself (2026-09-17 fix: a refresh used to drop your pick, so Approve could write an answer you had not chosen). A confirm stays armed across a refresh only for the option it was armed for and only inside its 8 seconds; if a card's options change, your pick and any armed confirm on it are dropped.

Resolved items live on the Timeline, not on this page. (2026-09-17 update — Jeremy asked for finished decisions off the main queue and into a visual changelog instead.) The queue view above only ever shows what's still open; saving a decision pops a brief confirmation toast linking straight to the Timeline, then the card disappears from the open list. Nothing is deleted or hidden — the Timeline is the complete, permanent history, grouped by the day it was resolved, newest day first. A card with followUpPending: true gets an amber marker instead of green on the Timeline, so an answer with unfinished work (an owner click, a build, a merge still pending) stays visually distinct even though it lives in the same place as everything else. "Resolved" and "done" are not the same word here.

Action cards (kind: "action") are clicks only Jeremy can make, and answering one runs nothing. (2026-09-17 owner decision, after action cards were marked Done three times in one day while GitHub and the disk showed no change.) They have no options and no preselected answer. The one button, I did it - check it, writes claimedAt (and claimComment) and leaves status: "open"; the card shows as Checking. A session then reads live state. If it took: the session sets status: "resolved", resolution (what was verified, with the evidence), verifiedBy, verifiedAt, resolvedAt. If it did not: the session clears claimedAt and writes checkResult, checkedBy, checkedAt, and the card returns to his list with that finding on top. This is the ONE case where a session writes resolved - only on an action card, only after his claim, only with recorded evidence. Put the steps in context (line breaks are kept): what to click, in what order, what he will see, how to undo. Any session that sees a card in Checking should verify it; the PM sweeps for them. If an action card does carry options (older cards, or one filed without kind whose first option is a completion claim), none of them is ever preselected and none can resolve it: "Done, ..." options fold into the one claim button (several of them are listed so he can say which in the box), "Not now" gets no button, and anything else ("It looked wrong") becomes a two-click report button that writes checkResult (the option text plus his comment), checkedBy: "owner", checkedAt, clears claimedAt, and leaves the card open for a session to pick up.

Never silently overwrite a resolved card. If a resolution needs correcting after the fact, append to its corrections array — { note, correctedBy, correctedAt, previousResolution } — and update resolution to the corrected value; don't just replace resolution in place. The card then shows both the current answer and the correction trail. Always pin writes to a resolved card with if_version and re-read on a conflict rather than forcing — Jeremy answers cards live, and a forced write can clobber his own answer.
